    The problem

    Dengue follows the rain.
    Reports follow the hospitals.

    Rain and heat create mosquito breeding sites. People get sick weeks later. Cases are only reported when they reach hospitals, often 4–8 weeks after conditions have already worsened.

    There's a gap between changing weather and hospital reports. EpiLag-PH helps close it.
    1. Week 0Rain and heat create breeding sites.
    2. +1–2 weeksMosquito populations increase.
    3. +2–4 weeksPeople get sick with dengue.
    4. +4–8 weeksCases are reported as hospitals see more patients.
    5. ThenHospital reports surge.
    6. ThenMore cases across the region.

    How scoring works

    A team covers about 33,000 people (up to 30% of a province) and prevents a share of the cases reported 2 to 9 weeks later, since people already infected still fall sick. The share uses cautious effects for clean-ups with container covers (10–50%, central 30%; Bowman et al. 2016). In 11 of 17 regions the computer player that follows EpiLag-PH averted more cases than the one that reacts to reports; about the same in 1, fewer in 5.
