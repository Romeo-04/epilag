EpiLag-PH: Forecasting Dengue Outbreaks 2–8 Weeks Ahead in the Philippines Using LightGBM Quantile Models and Weather Lags

Tolentino, J., Dacalan, P., & Gaytano, K.
Team DiagNodes · Philippine Junior Data Science Challenge (PJDSC) 2026 · Philippines
{{MOSQ}}
Problem

Dengue response in the Philippines usually starts after hospital admissions are already high. Cases are counted after people get sick, weeks after the rain that bred the mosquitoes, so clean-up drives and fogging often arrive after the peak.

Gap

Surveillance reports describe what already happened. A local health office rarely gets a forward-looking signal that says which area is heading for an outbreak, how sure that is, and what to do with a limited budget.

Solution · EpiLag-PH

EpiLag-PH forecasts weekly dengue cases 2–8 weeks ahead for 86 provinces, 17 regions and Quezon City, with a low–middle–high range. Each forecast becomes an alert level, a dated action checklist, a budget plan that ranks where each peso prevents the most cases, and a residents' outlook in Filipino or English. It runs free in the browser.

Did you know?About 442,000 dengue cases were reported in the Philippines in 2019 (DOH via HDX). Reported cases undercount infections, by about 4.7× in a Cebu City study.
The lag is the opportunityRain fills breeding sites weeks before mosquitoes multiply and people fall sick. That delay is time a health office can use.

Where skill comes from

Most skill comes from recent cases and the season. Adding rain, heat and humidity changed error by about −12% to +11%, by year and horizon.

The cost of early

Watch catches most starts but raises false alarms: 46% for provinces (2019), 42–63% for regions and QC. Warning is stricter.

Honest limits

Province data end Jan 2021 (replay only). Reported cases undercount; budget effects are ranges. It supports, not replaces, epidemiologists.
