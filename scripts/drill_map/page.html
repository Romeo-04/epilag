3D drill-down prototype for EpiLag. Click a region, then go deeper. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, right-drag to rotate.

    How the drill-down behaves

    Each click goes one level deeper. The camera flies to the new area and tilts in, the areas around it sink to the floor and turn grey, and the next level rises out of the ground as the camera arrives. Height and colour both show the alert level. Esc, the back button, a breadcrumb or a click on the sea goes back up.

    Motion

    • The camera follows the smooth zoom-out-pan-zoom-in curve (d3.interpolateZoom, van Wijk) and flattens its tilt by 10° mid-flight. Flights last 0.75–1.9 s depending on distance.
    • Child shapes rise at about half-way through the flight, with a slight overshoot, staggered up to 0.4 s by distance from the centre.
    • The selected outline settles in over 0.7 s. Search and the demo go down one level at a time so each layer is seen.
    • With reduced motion turned on, the camera jumps instead of flying.

    Porting to the app

    • The app already has MapLibre (lib/maplibre-map.js): fill-extrusion layers give the same 3D blocks, and flyTo with pitch uses the same curve.
    • Boundaries here come from the PSA PSGC shapefiles as published in faeldon/philippines-json-maps (hi-res pack, MIT licence), rebuilt by scripts/drill_map/boundaries.sh. Provinces are built by merging their municipalities, which keeps far more detail than the repo's app/data/province.geojson, where CAR and NCR are 5–17-point placeholders.
    • Barangay names are matched to the case table by name, since the two files use different PSGC codes.
    • The level is kept in the URL hash (try #qc), so a zoomed view can be shared.

    Gaps to close first

    • The province forecast in docs/web/data is from 10 Jan 2021, while the region one is from 28 Dec 2025. The panel labels the older date.
    • Barangays have no alert level of their own. They show expected cases on a separate blue scale.
    • The boundary set uses today's regions. Negros Island Region provinces are put back into Regions VI and VII, and Maguindanao del Norte and del Sur are merged into "Maguindanao (pre-2022)", to match the forecast units. The Special Geographic Area has no forecast unit and shows grey.
    • CAR is still the least detailed area (about 100 points per province) because the source itself is coarse there.
